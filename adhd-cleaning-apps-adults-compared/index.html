ADHD Cleaning Help for Adults, Compared: Free Fixes First, Then the Tools That Help

By the Kind Reset Team

"My house goes from perfect to a hoarders episode in a day or two." Then, as the same person on r/adhdwomen wrote: "Then comes the guilt. Then the shame." And: "I've tried setting the timers" (source).

If you've searched for an ADHD cleaning app, you've seen dozens. Many of them run into the same problem paper planners do: they need setup, they track streaks, and they show you a list of what you didn't do. So before looking at any tool, it helps to know which part of cleaning you're actually stuck on.

🔍 First, name your sticking point

Cleaning usually breaks down at one of four points. Each one needs a different kind of help.

Your sticking pointWhat it can look likeWhat helps
StartingYou stand and look at the mess and can't moveAnother person present (body doubling), a short timer
ChoosingEverything is a mess, so nothing feels like the place to beginA fixed order (trash → dishes → one surface)
Fitting it inCleaning never makes it into your actual dayA daily plan that puts chores next to work and appointments
Coming backYou missed a week and can't face itA no-guilt restart, no streaks

Pick the one or two that sound most like you. Ignore tools that solve the others.

🧠 How to try one without restarting the loop

A new app can be exciting for about a week. That's the pattern in why every ADHD planner works for a week. If you try one, set these rules on day one:

  1. Setup gets 10 minutes, max. If it needs more, it isn't the right fit right now.
  2. Add only three tasks, and make the first one small enough to start right now.
  3. Turn off streaks and overdue badges if the app allows it.
  4. Keep one line for after a missed day in the app's notes or on a sticky note: "I'm not starting over. I'm picking up where I am: one timer, three things."
  5. Set a reminder before the trial ends. Decide then, not on the day you get charged.

If you stop using it after two weeks, that's information, not failure. Go back to paper and the timer, which never expire.

🧹 The 10-minute reset, no app required

Whatever you pick, here's the routine underneath it, as it appears on page 1 of our sheet:

  1. Set a timer for 10 minutes. Grab one bag for trash. That's all the setup there is.
  2. Trash first. Walk the room with the bag. Anything that is clearly trash goes in. Skip anything you'd have to think about.
  3. Dishes second. Carry every cup, plate and bowl to the sink. Washing them is a job for another timer.
  4. One surface third. Pick the first table, counter or nightstand you see. Clear it. Things with a home go home. Everything else goes in one pile for later.
  5. When the timer rings, stop. Even mid-task. Stopping on time is part of the reset, and it can make the next one easier to start.
  6. Done counts: Tick a box after each reset. No dates, no streak. A gap is just a gap.

For the day after you skip it, see the morning-after restart script.

🩺 When to talk to someone

Apps and timers help with routines. They don't diagnose or treat anything. If the overwhelm feels constant, or the shame around mess is heavy, a doctor or licensed therapist is a good person to talk to.
